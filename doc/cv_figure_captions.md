# Draft caption: main cross-validation figure (R/fig_variance_explained.R, #36)

Numbers in [brackets] are from the October folds and the full-data fit behind the current two-stage maps; they are replaced by the final run (#35). Sources: `outputs/cv_level_skill.csv`, `outputs/cv_level_skill_by_insecticide.csv`, `outputs/cv_change_skill.csv`.

**Figure (`figures/CV_variance_explained.png`).**
Accuracy of bioassay-based estimates (green) and of the models (blue: dynamical model; magenta: two-stage model) for the level of mortality (top) and its change (bottom), with local data and without it.
Top row: the % of variance explained in single held-out bioassays, 100 (1 − MSE / Var(y)). (A) The interpolation test assays, predicted by the full-data fit, which included them. (B) The same assays, held out: no training data at their pixels, median [36] km from the nearest training assay of the same insecticide; [1,045] assays at [94] pixels. (C) Assays held out in contiguous sub-national blocks of six countries (two folds pooled), median [130] km from the nearest training assay of the same insecticide; [8,694] assays at [905] pixels. A and B score the same assays, so the step from A to B changes only whether the model saw the local data.
Bottom row: skill against no change, 100 (1 − Σ(P − A)² / ΣA²) (the persistence index; Kitanidis & Bras 1980). The target A is the change in mortality between a pair of single bioassays of one insecticide at one pixel, one in the five years before a forecast origin (2014 or 2018) and one in the five years after, at least 3 years apart, divided by the years between them; every prediction P of it is divided by the same gap. Each pixel-insecticide has equal weight. 0 is as accurate as assuming no change; below 0 is less accurate. [12,049] pairs ([68]% of all pairs; median gap [4] years) at [1,768] pixel-insecticides in [616] pixels. (D) The full-data fit, which included both assays of each pair. A pair of a 2013 and a 2018 assay enters at both origins; D scores it once ([11,644] distinct pairs). (E) Each forecast fold's model: fitted to the earlier assay, forecasting the later one. D and E score the same pairs, the repeats once in D.
Bars. *Local*: the expected score of a second, independent bioassay at the same pixel-year (level: 100 − 2 × the noise share), or of a second, independent pair at the same pixel (change), computed from the bioassay overdispersion ρ̂. *Best K*: the pooled K nearest training bioassays of the same insecticide from the two most recent years with data, at the K that minimises its own error on the held-out assays (a bound on any such rule, given hindsight the models are not). *Nearest*: the single nearest such bioassay. The models' bars score the map: the posterior mean of population mortality (for the two-stage model, without its site-level noise terms u and p).
Each bar is stacked as the key shows. The dotted line is the score of a perfect prediction of population mortality, given the target's beta-binomial bioassay noise at ρ̂: [78.0]% (A, B), [79.6]% (C), [48.7]% (D) and [48.8]% (E). Its low height for change is a finding: a single pair of bioassays carries little information about change. The local pair can score below 0 ([−1.9]%, D): it is right on average, but its large readings overstate the change.
n\*, the number of bioassays from the same pixel-year whose mean matches a bar's accuracy, is (100 − ceiling) / (ceiling − score): two-stage model [1.08] (B) and [0.47] (C); dynamical model [0.59] and [0.37]; best K [0.85] and [0.45]; in sample (A), two-stage [4.6] and dynamical [0.64]. Supplementary Tables [S?] give n\* from replicate bioassay pairs (#30).
Intervals are 95% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of pixels; each resample also takes a posterior draw of ρ by insecticide, which enters the dotted lines and the local bars. Dotted lines are drawn at their point estimates: [72.6–81.9]% (A, B), [77.5–81.2]% (C), [43.0–53.9]% (D), [42.9–53.8]% (E).
